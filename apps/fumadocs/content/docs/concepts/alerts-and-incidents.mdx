---
title: Alerts and incidents
description: The difference, and how one becomes the other.
---

## Alert

A possible change flagged by an agreed rule, for example a sharp rise in river turbidity. It carries:

- the readings behind it, each with its source and limits,
- a **confidence** level from the rule (low, medium, high),
- a **review status**.

An alert is a **prompt for review**. Most are checked and many are rejected: that's the system working.

## Incident

A **confirmed** alert routed to an institution for a response. It has:

- a **lead institution** that owns the case,
- **assignments** to institutions asked to act, with instructions and due dates,
- **actions** recorded (including a decision not to act),
- a **closing summary**.

An incident records an institutional response. **It is not a finding of wrongdoing** against any party.

## How one becomes the other

1. A rule flags an **alert**.
2. A person reviews it, maybe with a field check, and **confirms** it.
3. An analyst or regulator **escalates** it: an **incident** opens.
4. It's assigned, acted on and closed.
5. A **report** files the record.
