Success is **earlier, trusted action**, not the number of alerts. These measures come straight from the recorded workflow. They're **medians**, so one slow case doesn't hide the typical one. Choose the period: 30 days, 90 days, 12 months or pilot to date.

| Measure | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Detection latency** | From when a change was first observed to when it was flagged |
| **Time to decision** | From flagged to a person's recorded decision |
| **False-alert rate** | Rejected after review ÷ all decided alerts |
| **Response speed** | From the decision to the first documented action (including a decision not to act) |
| **Validation quality** | How alerts were decided: confirmed, rejected, unresolved, still waiting |
| **Coverage** | Priority sites with data in the last 30 days |
| **Adoption** | People active and actions recorded |
| **Completed cases** | Incidents closed; field reports and reports filed |
| **Decision usefulness** | Share of readers who said a report helped a decision |

<Callout type="info">
While the pilot uses prepared data, these figures describe the demonstration, not the field. The dashboard says so.
</Callout>

## Other widgets

- **Incidents:** active, overdue and closed, by lead institution.
- **Activity compared with concessions:** land and water alerts inside a concession, within 1 km, away from any, or in a reserve. Boundaries are illustrative; neither inside nor outside is proof of legality.
- **Rules under review:** each rule's rejection rate. A high rate is a reason for scientists to revisit its threshold.
